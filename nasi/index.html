The Daily Nasi · הנשיא

For the first thirteen days of the Hebrew month of Nissan, it is customary to read each day the Torah passage of that day’s Nasi, the prince of one of the tribes of Israel, followed by a short prayer, the Yehi Ratzon. Here is everything you need: what it is, when and how to say it, and the full text for every day.

What is the Nasi?

The Mishkan, the portable Sanctuary the Jewish people built in the desert, was set up on the first day of Nissan, one year after the Exodus from Egypt. For the next twelve days, the leader (Nasi, plural Nesiim) of each of the twelve tribes brought a dedication offering for the altar, one tribe each day. The Torah records every one of them in full, in Numbers (Bamidbar) chapter 7, in Parshat Naso.

Each prince brought exactly the same gift: a silver dish and a silver bowl filled with fine flour mixed with oil, a gold spoon filled with incense, animals for a burnt-offering, a goat for a sin-offering, and oxen, rams, goats and lambs for a peace-offering. Even so, the Torah repeats the whole list for every prince, teaching that each brought it with his own intention and his own meaning.

The custom is to read each Nasi on the day of Nissan on which he brought his offering. The custom is mentioned by the Shelah HaKadosh and is followed in many communities; in Chabad it is printed in the siddur together with the Yehi Ratzon, and it is widely kept.

When and how is it said?

  • Days: 1 Nissan through 13 Nissan, every day including Shabbat. In 5787 that is Thursday, 8 April 2027 to Tuesday, 20 April 2027.
  • Time: by day, usually right after Shacharit (the morning prayers).
  • What: the verses of that day’s prince, read from a Chumash or siddur, then the Yehi Ratzon (days 1–12).
  • Day 13: the summary of all the offerings and the passage of the Menorah, for the tribe of Levi, without a Yehi Ratzon.

The Yehi Ratzon prayer · יהי רצון

After the Nasi on each of the first twelve days, a short prayer is said. It asks that, if the person praying is from the tribe whose prince was read that day, all the holy sparks and lights of that tribe shine on him, helping him understand the Torah, fear G-d and do His will, together with his children and grandchildren.

Since most people today do not know their tribe, the prayer is said every day. The Chabad custom is that everyone says it, Kohanim and Levi’im included. On 13 Nissan there is no Yehi Ratzon.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Nasi?

The Nasi (Hebrew נשיא, “prince”) is the daily reading, during the first days of Nissan, of the Torah passage in Numbers chapter 7 describing the offering one of the twelve tribal princes brought for the dedication of the Mishkan (Tabernacle). Each day of Nissan has its own Nasi.

When is the Nasi said?

Every day from 1 Nissan to 13 Nissan, including Shabbat. Most people say it after the morning prayers (Shacharit). Days 1–12 each have a tribe’s prince; on day 13 the closing passage for the tribe of Levi is read.

When is the Nasi in 2027?

In 5787 the Nasi is said from Thursday, 8 April 2027 (1 Nissan) to Tuesday, 20 April 2027 (13 Nissan).

Why is the Nasi said in Nissan?

The Mishkan was set up on 1 Nissan, and for the next twelve days the princes of the twelve tribes each brought a dedication offering, one prince per day. Reading each day’s offering on its anniversary connects us with the dedication. Because each of those days was a festival for that tribe, Tachanun is not said all month.

What is the Yehi Ratzon said after the Nasi?

A short prayer asking that the holy sparks and lights of that day’s tribe shine on the person saying it, “if I, Your servant, am of the tribe” of that day, helping him understand Torah and serve G-d. It is said after the Nasi on days 1–12; on 13 Nissan the Nasi is said without a Yehi Ratzon.

What is read on 13 Nissan?

Numbers 7:84 to 8:4: the summary of all twelve offerings (“Zot Chanukat HaMizbeach”) and the first verses of Behaalotecha about lighting the Menorah. It corresponds to the tribe of Levi, whose share was Aharon’s lighting of the Menorah.

Where does the Nasi text come from?

The Hebrew is the text of the Torah, Numbers chapter 7. The Yehi Ratzon is printed in the siddur. The English translations on this site are reproduced with permission from Kehot Publication Society.
